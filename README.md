# CERE

**CERE** — spelled C-E-R-E, for the **cerebellum**, pronounced "Sarah."

A predictive wraparound for the brain of [Duality](https://github.com/k77722420-stack/Duality-AI): a governed pattern-memory layer that learns how its owner reaches conclusions, and — in shadow mode first — tests whether that history can forecast the owner's later confirmed choices.

The cerebellum does not decide where you are going. It predicts where your own motion will land, compares the prediction against reality, and corrects the difference. That is CERE's job description, and its limit: **CERE predicts and corrects. It never authors, never acts, and never speaks before the owner decides.**

## The claim, in its testable form

> Given what was knowable at a specified decision point, can governed personal history improve a forecast of the person's later confirmed choice?

That is the whole claim. It is tested against two controls — a statistics-only model and a generic-user model — under predeclared gates, with sealed forecasts, human verdicts, and full calibration scoring. A clean negative result is a success of the method.

## Status — November 23, 2026

Design phase complete. The blueprint, the full architecture and mathematics, an independent design verdict (adopted in full), a senior operations audit, and a binding enforcement charter are all in this repository. **No study code exists yet, and no study data lives here — ever.** The study itself runs on the owner's own machine, under the owner's control.

## Foundations

CERE stands on published work, credited: Microsoft Research's UserLM line (user simulation from intent and history), UserRepGen (structured behavior profiles), the Generative Agents memory-stream architecture (Park et al.), and theory-of-mind evaluation work such as SimpleToM. The combination — owner-controlled eligible memory, independent human verdicts, enforceable use limits — is the project's own.

The hat monotile that seals CERE's profile versions is credited to Smith, Myers, Kaplan & Goodman-Strauss. CERE claims only the use, never the shape.
